# Character, Dialogue, Exposition, Subtext

Source: John Yorke, *Into the Woods*.

**Master principle: a thing juxtaposed with its opposite:** Character = facade vs flaw; Structure = want vs need; Dialogue = word vs deed; Exposition = information vs conflict; Subtext = text vs intention. Character and structure are indivisible (one manifests the other). Correctly juxtaposed opposites explode into living story.

## Character vs characterization

- **Identical yet different:** all share the same make-up (love, jealousy, procreation, defensiveness, openness, vengeance, kindness) in different proportions; everyone customizes everything uniquely, the raw material of characterization.
- **Revealed through choice under pressure:** each scene = a mini-crisis (subverted expectation / blow to plans) forcing a choice that reveals who they are. Stop moving = stop being revealed = audience stops caring. Same £10m, each spends differently; every obstacle leaves unique fingerprints. *Tinker Tailor* opening: four entrances characterize wordlessly via a trivial shared obstacle. *House of Saddam* opening: Saddam shoots his best friend (ruthlessness) yet loves his family: paradox = truth.
- **Root of all character = image at war with feeling** (wished perception vs real feeling). Nixon (Stone): "they see what they want to be; me, what they are." Test: honest vs public answer: the gap IS the character. **Animal beneath citizen:** rational animals restricting feeling to live in society; anonymity lets the animal (impotence, anger, rage) off the leash; public virtue is universal (no one backed Vichy). Perception-management drains energy, fuels fashion/art and its inverse ("guilty pleasures"). Real dissonance: Stalin "loved" the millions he killed; Blair/socialism-vs-capitalism; Kennedy's idealism excluded women/Vietnam; Jobs the anti-materialist; solitary Reagan. Montaigne: "double in ourselves." St Paul: "the good I would, I do not." **Consequence:** characters are truest when they lie, self-sabotage, act against their beliefs. *Butch Cassidy* cliff: fearless Sundance finally confesses "I can't swim!", full character (invulnerable image, hidden shame, safe confession to a partner) in an action beat; McKee: they "are one protagonist." Chris Rock: "you're meeting their representative."
- **Psychological basis (why the paradox is universal):**
  - **Maslow (1943):** hierarchy of needs: primal urges (food, water, sex, security, self-esteem, self-actualization) are selfish survival drives, but safety demands REPRESSING them to live in a group; that repression = the image/feeling gap.
  - **Freud:** super-ego (rational parent) vs id (animal child). **Jung:** persona (facade) vs shadow (unconscious urges) which it must integrate. **Erikson:** normative conflict / identity crisis. **Adler:** inferiority complex. **Rollo May:** existential psychoanalysis. All agree: man lives conflicted (primal desire vs acceptable behaviour); neuroses must be integrated/overcome for "happiness."
  - This IS the archetypal story: flawed hero → completion by integrating lessons; happy ending = resolved conflict, balanced opposites. Fitzgerald: hold two opposed ideas and still function.
- **Build every major character on a paradox** (Goodyear: "a lid on a scream"): House (misanthrope who saves lives); Brent (believes all love him, hiding loneliness; fake-doc format shows both self + facade); Basil Fawlty (refined man / philistine); Mainwaring (respectable soldier / provincial figure of fun); Don Draper (rich sophisticate / poor lost "white trash," not even Draper); Corleone (at war with himself); Zuckerberg (loneliness + rage in the score); **Glee vs Mad Men** (mirror: liberate inner self vs repress all feeling for appearance). Conflict can **split across two characters** (Butch & Sundance, buddy-cop) or one into two identities (Superman, Spider-Man, Captain America, Batman, Hulk as dark inversion, Harry Potter, Dexter): familiar figure hiding an omnipotent god.

## Want, need, facade, flaw (character = structural design)

- **Want**: conscious desire for what they THINK completes them; the wished-for image (Kane: power; Gatsby: wealth; Soprano: "respect"; Rocky: title; McQueen: Piston Cup). Bolsters the mask/super-ego. (Facebook = adverts for the wished-for self.)
- **Need**: unconscious deeper requirement that makes them whole; they fear loss would expose the soft id: the very part that would complete them.
- **Facade**: traits believed beneficial that actually destroy them. **The facade triggers the problem** (McQueen's arrogance; Thacker's shyness; Baker's selfishness; Corleone's compassion → avenging his father).
- **Flaw / hidden strength**: the "weakness" becomes the redemption (McQueen's empathy saves him; in dark inversion, Corleone's ruthlessness secures succession).
- **Antagonist = embodiment of the very thing the protagonist lacks;** the "monster" externalizes the internal gap.

| Character | Want | Facade (believed good) | Flaw/buried trait | Need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| McQueen | Piston Cup | arrogance, indifference | selflessness, empathy | connection, humility |
| Thacker | Anna | diffidence, shyness, reserve | capacity to act/declare | courage to claim love |
| Kane | power | empire builder | boy deprived of love | to love & be loved |
| Gatsby (tragedy) | Daisy via wealth | invented self | poor boy underneath | authenticity (never embraced) |
| Corleone (dark inversion) | stay clean of family | war-hero civilian | ruthlessness | flaw "wins": succession |

- **Facade/flaw iceberg:** above waterline = presentation (WANT feeds the image; FACADE = beneficial-seeming traits that destroy). Below = true engine, hidden from others AND self (FLAW = the weakness holding redemption; NEED = unconscious wholeness). Drama = pressure forcing the submerged part up.
- **Egri's seeds:** "every character must have the seeds of its future development": plant villainy/salvation in the first scenes (Baker minds the girl, fights for his dog, "great hands"; Corleone's STEEL explaining the family to Kay). Conflict = tinder ready to catch.
- **Arc across five acts (learn cold):** (1) need becomes **conscious at the inciting incident**; (2) **embraced end of act 2**; (3) **triumphs over want first at the midpoint** (a 3-D-midpoint definition: Thelma's post-coital revelation; Corleone's pain+power; subconscious dredged up); (4) **post-midpoint: integrate** the new dominant self WITH the good of the old (not simply become new); (5) **the two sides balance**: thesis + antithesis → synthesis.
- **Proportions (25/75), a tool not maths:** Thelma starts 25% woman/75% child, ends reversed; the mix shifts act by act (Elle Woods' costume tracks it; Macbeth's poison grows). **Law:** apparent quirks = the war of opposites seeking symmetry: qualities pursued toward the goal sabotage it; their unconscious opposites bring salvation if redemption is the endpoint.
- **Tragedy = the reversal:** cannot trade ego-goal for satisfying need → punished. Gatsby: the fictitious self built to win Daisy pushes her away. Ziggy Sobotka (*Wire* s2): inept son proving himself a "major-league criminal," derision worsening the need, cannot reconcile want with reality → tragic.

## Character individuation

- **One flaw, infinite characters:** people build a public face to manage inner conflict (skyscraper/nun/comic; *Priest*'s gay priest; *Boardwalk Empire*'s deviant enforcer). If every vulnerability just grew a hard shell, few types would exist, yet *Witness*/*Searchers*/*Notting Hill* all centre on intimacy-terrified men (Book, Edwards, Thacker), worlds apart. Two answers:
  1. **Dress** differently: quirks, language, milieu, setting.
  2. **Conflict manifests through different ego defence mechanisms.** Wants = things mistakenly believed to complete them (Kane controls a terrifying world; Gatsby buries poverty; Soprano masks vulnerability with violence; Draper buries his true self).
- **Ego defence mechanisms:** id impulses (sex, revenge) collide with super-ego → anxiety → the ego builds defences (coping, not happiness). Tynan: "a neurosis is a secret you don't know you're keeping." Catalogued by Sigmund & Anna Freud in four categories: **Pathological** (delusional projection), **Immature** (passive aggression), **Neurotic** (hypochondriasis), **Mature** (humour, altruism). The ten with clear dramatic manifestations:

| Mechanism | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Intellectualization | focus on non-emotive aspects | *Manhattan* |
| Repression | repel pleasurable instincts | *Remains of the Day* |
| Regression | retreat to an earlier stage | *The Big Chill* |
| Sublimation | shift negative emotion into another object | *Chocolat* |
| Rationalization | specious reasoning-away of trauma | *Leaving Las Vegas* |
| Isolation | separate feeling from ideas/events | *The Searchers* |
| Projection | attack others for a fault within | *The Sopranos* |
| Denial | refuse to acknowledge trauma | *Rosemary's Baby* |
| Displacement | shift aggression to another target | *Death Wish* |
| Reaction formation | believe the opposite of what one feels | *Crash* |

- **Individuation question:** for a shared flaw (can't be intimate), ask how THIS one reacts: intellectualize? blame the partner? joke? isolate? bury desire? deny? act childishly? use prostitutes? build a skyscraper? attack? Then dress it. The chosen defence = the mask worn on first meeting, sloughing away if the archetype completes (a template for resolving neurotic conflict).
- **Rubber Ducky moment (origin of the flaw):** all 3-D characters start flawed (want/need mismatch, damaging defences); trauma often off-screen before the story.
  - **Term:** Lumet & Chayefsky's cynical slang ("someone stole their rubber ducky as a baby"), cynical but a real-pedigree motif.
  - Formula: Kane deprived of love → empire; Louise hard because of Texas; Rick selfish because Ilsa left Paris. **Canonical:** *Thelma & Louise* (Louise raped, Texas); *Kane* ("Rosebud"); *Casablanca* (Ilsa, Paris); *Rain Man* (brothers split as babies); *Silence of the Lambs* (slaughtered lambs); *EastEnders* (Kat's rape by Uncle Harry); *Walk the Line*/*Ray!* (sibling's early death).
  - **Placement:** usually withheld to at least end of act 4, for mystery, and because the story IS the journey to confront the cause; it's the schism between the healthy self born and the damaged self formed, the event that catalysed the defences and facade.
  - **Periodic structure:** foundation of films where change happens not IN the character but by slow revelation TO US (change in us): *I Know Where I'm Going!*, *Red Road*, *Rachel Getting Married*, *Bourne Supremacy*.
  - **Warnings:** at worst = melodrama/speechifying. Mamet's "death of my kitten" speech (¾-through). Stephens: drunken confessional revealed 4/5 through = "theatrically inert." Welles: "Rosebud" = "dime-book Freud"; Quint's USS Indianapolis speech walks the line.
  - **Rule:** off-screen trauma works when it informs the current storyline and creates an active goal (*Thelma & Louise*), poorly as mere back-story. *Social Network* = one long argument over what the moment even is.
- **Case for minimal backstory:** Mamet: narrative-not-dramatic stops the show; only interest = what's next. 23 Bond films tell us nothing (pure character). Less backstory → easier identification (Tintin = blank for a child to inhabit). Fincher on Bickle: "I don't know and I don't care, that's what makes him compelling." Archetypal drama induces peace (a balm, purging our distress by enacting the process); badly-placed backstory disrupts empathy. If character and structure are one, no explanation is needed.

## Dialogue and characterization

- **Every utterance = an expression of intent.** The three functions (characterization, exposition, subtext) are all products of desire; dialogue is born of and a component of structure.
- Hare: "telling not showing" is truer, but only because **telling IS showing** (dialogue reveals character), not because film is verbal art.
- **Kuleshov Effect applies to dialogue** (base soup/coffin/girl case: scene-craft.md "Showing, not telling"; realized with sound, *Jazz Singer* 1927): audiences infer causal links between image and dialogue. Dialogue builds the facade (guards up); masks drop when words are juxtaposed with contrary action: the gap makes the audience active.
- **Good dialogue = a manifestation of behaviour, not an explanation of it.** Speaking = doing, a tool to negotiate an obstacle; each utterance leaves a fingerprint. Dialogue = the character IN action.
- **Not conversation** (real speech = a mess; good dialogue = the ILLUSION of it, subservient to structure) **and not narrative** (doesn't carry the story; = the characters' REACTIONS to its obstacles).
- **Whedon's law:** everyone (even the villain's thug) has a reason, voice, identity, history. Speaking only to set up the next line = soundbites, not dialogue.
- **Voice differentiation axes:** grammar, vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, sentence length, jargon/slang: change one → change the character. Not WHAT but HOW: each choice reveals the crucible (desire, culture, background, worldview, status, social codes, gender, subconscious fears, upbringing), often subconscious but active. Dialogue conveys the wished-for image while betraying hidden flaws; comedy exaggerates (Brent; Ali G).
- **Red flags:** dwelling on backstory / over-explanation; "do you remember?" / "that reminds me of when..." = a piece in trouble; dialogue fails when it stops being a characterized reaction to an obstacle. Mercurio: dialogue is least important, new writers over-polish it to fix what is really a structure/character problem. Structure is the bedrock.

## Exposition

TELLING in a SHOWING medium: form vs function; rare in life, so easiest to write badly, a great tell of talent when mastered.

- **History:** Euripides' **Prologue** (a deity narrator) delivered all setting/biography/motivation → realism made an outside character anomalous (Shakespeare's passing gentlemen; Victorian "table-dusting" gossiping maids) → the device died once craft could present info dramatically/surreptitiously. **Rattigan** = first master (*Winslow Boy*: theft, trial, national scandal in reported action without leaving the drawing room). Cautionary: early UK drama (*Holby* 2005) over-explained everything believing the audience dim, turgid.
- **Core dilemma:** demand plausibility (no one says what they never would) yet impart essential info: square it via dramatic structure.
- **Two types:** (1) info neither audience nor characters know: reason can be explicit; (2) info the audience needs but ALL characters already know: dangerous; the reason must be underlying, i.e. SHOWN.
- **Techniques:**
  - **Questioner device** (type 1): doctors/policemen/an ingenue (Carter/*ER*, Rachel/*Friends*, Sam/*Life on Mars*, *Casualty* first-years) legitimately elicit plot info; tourists/students/strangers/authority = us. *EastEnders* opened (1985) with a murder so interrogation gave access to the Square.
  - **Reason through desire** (type 2): not "you know you've got..." but "See a doctor, it's cancer": desperation supplies a REASON = desire. All exposition, like all dialogue, is desire-driven; bad = that imperative absent.
  - **Disguise with conflict:** desire countered by opposite desire (Westminster demo: flat "Isn't that the Prince Regent who consorts with prostitutes?"/"Yes" vs two men arguing whether he disgraces the Queen: same facts, now invisible). **Rule: exposition works as a tool a character uses to achieve desire; more conflict = less visible.**
  - **Impact:** stronger if the policeman talks to the culprit, the doctor hates bad news, or the speaker is the subject's secret lover.
  - **Emotional overlay:** Mercurio (*Cardiac Arrest*): a terrified junior doctor stammers a death notice so painfully the widow still asks "how is Albert now?"; emotion renders exposition undetectable.
  - **Move or cut:** deliver each fact where it's genuinely NEW to someone on screen, consequences later; the *Holby* rewrite reduces needed info (emergency ultrasound) to near-wordless (nurse spots anomaly, patient worried, cut to ultrasound).
- **The *Apocalypse Now* briefing (all techniques married):** conveys who Kurtz was, what he became, the mission ("terminate... with extreme prejudice"), the moral rot, via: every line a clear (often masked) intention; the denial as a TEST (Willard's eyes reveal he did it, word vs image = subtext); conflict/unease underneath (commanders do the same dirty tricks); the deeply personal (Kurtz's taped voice; "a conflict in every human heart between the rational and the irrational") for impact + emotional overlay. Study for any briefing/mission scene carrying heavy info.

## Subtext

- **Definition/source:** Cronkite on Kennedy's death: flat phrase + action (glasses off, pause, effortfully replaced) lets us infer the epic news (Kuleshov; power in what's concealed). Tally: "not the emotion they're playing but the emotion they're trying to conceal." Stanislavski: seek an underlying objective in every line: meaning lies in a battle for status/control/power or suppressed love/sadness/loss. **Subtext emerges from the interaction between a character's facade and their actual intention;** as want collides with need under pressure the mask slips, and the word/deed gap is the drama. Extreme: Little Mo's "I love you" to her rapist = an active desire for safety, not truth.
- **Safety governs masking:** each character enters with a desire; openness depends on whom/state/where. All seek safety: safe to declare, or a safer route? Safety is subjective (highest/lowest status, only with a spouse). Security → overt; insecurity (rival, violent husband, new girlfriend) → masked = subtext. When desire is fully overt (Corleone deciding to kill Sollozzo) there's no subtext and none needed.
- **Masking ladder**: same intent (seduction), rising mask, constant intent, more inference = more character: (1) "I'm going to take your clothes off" (zero mask); (2) "I really like you" (light); (3) "Bit chilly, isn't it?" (heavy deflection); (4) "Think you're special, don't you?" (inverted into aggression, implying a hinterland of troubles).
- **Two facades:** (1) **conscious deception of others** (*Ladykillers* gang; Ash in *Alien*); (2) **unconscious self-deception**: richer, product of greater conflict + its defence (*Curse of Steptoe*: Corbett can't admit he just makes people laugh, unaware of the masked desire).
- Simmel: we communicate only "a selection" of the psychological whole. No subtext = flat literal world; subtext plays in the gap between language and thought, closer to truth.
- **Rules/warnings:**
  - **Subtext is not elitist; it's the hook**: interpretation makes the audience active (any IQ reads a nurse's "everything's fine" when it isn't). Replacing meaningful lines with literal drains the blood from a work.
  - **Never let characters explain their own motives**: sounds false; only the pathological let truth shine through the facade (Montaigne: "no description is as difficult as the description of self"). Self-knowledge = the ENDPOINT, not an exposition tool.
  - **Audiences infer intention, not every word**: no surgery needed to enjoy *ER* (just the goal); decode from context; *The Sting*'s con-patter explains nothing and works. Simon: spoon-feeding makes every character talk alike (*EastEnders* once banned regional/cockney accents, forgetting *Auf Wiedersehen, Pet*/*Minder* revelled in dialect).
  - **Voice-over almost always fails**: the narrator tells everything, no subtext (*Pushing Daisies*, *A Very Long Engagement*, *One From the Heart*).
  - **Exception: unreliable narrators:** Welles in *F For Fake*; *Gulliver's Travels*: the gap between what Gulliver does (living in a stable with a horse) and his "rational" narration dramatizes his unrecognized madness. The gap IS the drama (Kuleshov again).
- **Endpoint: King Lear:** condemning Cordelia for speaking as she feels, Lear learns the cost of the speech/thought gap. Protagonists heal the duality (inner/outer, want/need, facade/flaw, finally word/deed). "Speak what we feel, not what we ought to say" (Edgar) reconciles facade with truth. Dialogue, masking as much as revealing, is as indispensable to structure as character, act division, inciting incident and story.

## Quick checklist

1. Each major character: facade, hidden flaw, defence mechanism? Paradox statable in one sentence (House)?
2. Revealed through choice under pressure every scene? Characterizable from how they enter a room (Tinker Tailor)?
3. Want (conscious, image-driven) vs need: surfaces at inciting incident, embraced end act 2, triumphs first at midpoint, integrates post-midpoint, balances at close. Tragedy = fails the trade, punished.
4. Antagonist = embodiment of what the protagonist lacks.
5. Egri's seeds: saving/damning traits planted visibly in the first scenes?
6. Same flaw, different mask chosen (intellectualize/blame/joke/isolate/deny/regress/sublimate/attack), then dressed distinctly?
7. Rubber Ducky: revealed trauma informs the storyline + creates an active goal (not back-story)? Withheld to end of act 4? Considered revealing nothing (Bond, Bickle)?
8. Every speaker (incl. henchman) has a reason, voice, identity, history; none just sets up another's line.
9. Voices differentiated (grammar/vocab/syntax/rhythm/length/jargon); dialogue = reaction to obstacle, never transcript, never story-carrier.
10. Red flags absent: no "do you remember?", no "that reminds me of...", no over-polishing dialogue to fix a structure/character problem.
11. Exposition: each fact a tool of desire, countered by opposing desire, with impact + emotional overlay; else moved to where it's new on screen, or cut to near-wordlessness.
12. Every scene runs on masked intention scaled by safety; the mask slips only under pressure.
13. No character explains their own motives; self-knowledge only at the end. No omniscient voice-over (unreliable narrators excepted).
