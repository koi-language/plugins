# Worked Examples: Act Structures of Real Stories

Source: John Yorke, *Into the Woods*, Appendices I-VII. Worked demonstrations of the five-act shape, the roadmap of change, act mirroring, and the equivalence of rival structure systems. None was written to a template; the template is what you find when you X-ray finished work, because the shape is natural. "Very few works fit with total accuracy" (Yorke on Hamlet), yet works centuries apart share one skeleton.

## How to use these (calibration for story-structure.md)

1. **Act count is elastic in the middle, fixed at the edges.** Raiders proves the middle holds as many acts as needed (7 + coda). Never moves: act one = a complete mini-story; midpoint truth at the exact center; worst point near the end of the penultimate act; final act mirrors the first.
2. **Locate the midpoint first**: where the protagonist touches the story's "truth" (proof of guilt, first kill, first drink, finding the Ark, the sex inside Malkovich). Before = journey toward it; after = paying for it. Want gives way to need.
3. **Check the worst point** at end of act four (or proportional equivalent). In tragedy it inverts into the highpoint of ambition (Being John Malkovich).
4. **Run the roadmap of change** (15 beats). Expect misfits (Yorke flags them in Hamlet); the overarching shape matters, not beat-by-beat compliance.
5. **Mirror first and last acts** (Appendix VI). If the ending doesn't answer the opening, the opening is wrong.
6. **Translate gurus, don't fight them** (Appendix VII): every system is the same engine in different clothes.

### Generic five-act shape (proportions matter more than page numbers)

The named-beat timeline (inciting incident late in act 1, fires the story question; midpoint truth at the exact center, want→need; worst point at the end of act 4, consequences of NOT changing made as bad as possible, often a death; climax in act 5 as the obligatory showdown answering act 1) is drawn in story-structure.md "Five-act timeline with named beats". Proportions matter more than page numbers.

### Roadmap of change (15 beats, 3/act; see story-structure.md for the beat table), applied below

**Dark inversion** (The Godfather, Macbeth): identical beats, reversed polarity: "mastery" = the flaw has consumed the character.

## I. Raiders of the Lost Ark: 7 acts + coda

Proves the middle expands into as many acts as needed. Each act = a contained gain / loss / regain of the treasure, ending in a turn that launches the next desire.
1 Peru: Indy takes the idol; rival Belloq steals it, leaves him for dead. 2 USA: army tasks him to find the Ark before the Nazis (mission given). 3 Nepal: Marion holds the headpiece to the Staff of Ra; Nazis burn her tavern; the two escape and hunt the Ark together. 4 Cairo: with the real headpiece Indy finds the **true Ark location (MIDPOINT)**; Nazis seize it, seal the pair in the Well of Souls; they escape. 5 Airstrip: convoy chase, Indy recovers the Ark. 6 Steamer: Belloq's sub steals it again, kidnaps Marion; Indy stows away. 7 Island: bluff fails; tied to a post (**WORST POINT**), forced to watch the Ark opened; he shuts his eyes, demonic forces destroy the Nazis, Ark restored (**CLIMAX**). Coda: Ark lost among identical warehouse crates.

**Act one = complete mini-story** (fractal principle: every act and scene replicates the whole): protagonist Indy / antagonist Belloq / inciting incident = finding the temple / desire = retrieve the idol past every trap / crisis = Belloq's ambush at the exit / climax = Belloq steals it, leaves him for dead / resolution = escape by seaplane.

## II. Hamlet: the metaphor for all of them

Each of its five acts has a turning point launching a new desire. Structurally a **detective story**: first half asks "Did Claudius murder the king?", second half reacts to the answer found exactly halfway: "He did." **Misfits Yorke flags:** Ophelia's death lands at end of act 4 as expected, but Hamlet has *already* recommitted after Fortinbras's army. Beats overlap rather than queue. Parallels reach even to Thelma & Louise, proving the shape's universality.

1 Ghost reveals Claudius's murder, demands revenge. 2 Paralysed by doubt; learns R&G spy; resolves to test Claudius via players ("The play's the thing"). 3 The staging makes Claudius's reaction confirm guilt (**MIDPOINT**); Hamlet kills Polonius. 4 Claudius plots his murder abroad; Fortinbras's army spurs Hamlet to recommit; Ophelia drowns (**WORST POINT**). 5 Rigged fencing match: poison kills Laertes, Gertrude, Claudius, Hamlet; Fortinbras succeeds; Hamlet declared a hero; order restored.

Roadmap: unaware of ghost → learns → sees → doubt ("lost all my mirth") → R&G are spies → "the play's the thing" → trains players → **BIG CHANGE: proves guilt** → kills Polonius, banished → departs → learns of murder plan → Regression (Fortinbras; Ophelia dies) → Yorick/Laertes → kills Claudius, all slain → Total mastery: dies knowing his story lives on.

## III. Being John Malkovich: iconoclastic yet archetypal

108 min, near-symmetrical acts, explicit "into the woods" (a journey into another place to find the missing part of oneself). **Theme stated by Maxine at min 51:** the world divides into those who chase what they want and those who don't; the passionate stay vital, die with fewer regrets. Craig journeys to escape his old self, with terrible consequences. **Tragic riff:** the worst point inverts into the highpoint of ambition: Craig gets exactly what he wanted at the moment his doom is sealed.

1 Failed puppeteer Craig takes a filing job, fixates on Maxine. 2 Finds the portal into Malkovich's head; Maxine plans to sell access ($200/turn); Lotte tries it, is transformed. 3 Lotte, inside John, sleeps with Maxine at min 54 (**MIDPOINT**); jealous Craig cages Lotte, enters John, takes her place. 4 Lester reveals the portal grants eternal youth; Craig decides to inhabit Malkovich full-time as a famous puppeteer with Maxine, his downfall plan (**WORST POINT**: unaware of the forces against him = ambition highpoint). 5 Craig-as-John succeeds, marries Maxine; Lester's gang forces him out; Lotte (as John) fathered Maxine's child; Craig ends imprisoned inside the daughter for 40 years: **total dark mastery of another person, just not the one he expected.**

Roadmap follows the standard 15 beats (Awakening = finds portal; Acceptance = business with Maxine; BIG CHANGE = the sex + fury; Regression = full-time-possession plan; Total mastery = domination achieved).

## IV. My Zinc Bed: reconciliation of opposites

David Hare's addiction study built entirely on **reconciliation of opposites** (passion/repression, feeling/intensity, living/surviving), a line back to Sophocles' Antigone; juxtaposing and assimilating two polarized views is both form and content. A character quotes the dialectic (Jung: loving another compensates for a lack in ourselves, can never complete us); Victor's margarita, "cold as hell in the mouth, hot as hell going down: contradiction at the heart of life," is the theme: the play a metaphor for structure itself. **Form note:** written as two acts of six scenes; Yorke re-presents it in five to show the skeleton stays identical under any construct.

1 Recovering-alcoholic poet Paul is hired by millionaire Victor. 2 Meets Elsa (recovering addict, Victor's wife); after arguing whether AA is a cult, they kiss. 3 Paul takes his first drink, fused symbolically with love (**MIDPOINT** = end of Part One); drunk, vows to renounce drink if he can have Elsa. 4 Victor suspects the affair; his marriage and business fail; Paul returns to AA, quits, leaves. 5 Elsa begs; he answers that loving her is just another addiction, and moves on. Victor later dies drunk in a crash. Paul, ambiguously, has learned to live again.

Roadmap: standard 15 beats. Paul moves repression → passion → understanding in between (ambiguous healing); BIG CHANGE = drink-as-declaration; Total mastery = Paul leaves, no longer dependent.

## V. The Godfather: modern tragedy / dark inversion

A hero spiritually dead, following the change paradigm with reversed polarity. At the midpoint Michael faces the opposite of all he believed and pulls the trigger; opposites fuse, a new dark character is born; every later kill destroys a better part of him.

1 Connie's wedding; Michael tells Kay "That's my family, not me." The Don is shot. 2 Michael thwarts a hospital hit, finds it exhilarating; McCluskey breaks his jaw. 3 Michael kills McCluskey and Sollozzo (**MIDPOINT**); Sicily, marries, wife murdered; Sonny killed; the Don sues for peace. 4 Michael promises Kay legitimacy; pressure from rival families and Moe Greene; the Don dies warning the traitor will offer to broker a deal (**WORST POINT**: alone, surrounded, father gone). 5 Tessio makes the offer (traitor revealed); christening massacre: Barzini, Greene, the Dons, Carlo, Tessio killed; Michael lies to Kay about Carlo (**CLIMAX**).

Roadmap identical in shape to Hamlet's, only polarity differs: **Total mastery = mastery of evil, the flaw has consumed the man** (head of the family; the lie to Kay).

## VI. First-and-last-act parallels (the mirror principle)

Act one's beats echo, inverted, in act five, measuring how far the protagonist travelled. The pattern is **chiastic** (A-B-C answered by C-B-A): opening beat rhymes with closing beat, inward. Write these two columns first; if they don't answer each other, the story doesn't yet know what it's about.

- **The King's Speech.** dreadful speech→seeks Logue | refuses to talk→insists he has a voice | rejects Logue→makes a brilliant speech.
- **Fabulous Baker Boys.** "See you again?" "No"→turns Monica down | cool facade, loathes brother→drops it, embraces brother | meets/pursues Suzy→"See you again?" "Maybe?"
- **The Social Network** (perfect chiasmus). dumped by Erica→twins sue him | Facemash + enlists Eduardo→Facebook + sacks Eduardo | twins invite him in→he tries to "friend" Erica.
- **The Godfather.** sacrament (wedding, Don grants favours)→sacrament (christening, dispenses death) | war hero outsider→the Don, business incarnate | "That's my family, not me" (truth)→"Did you kill Carlo?" "No" (lie).
- **Thelma and Louise.** both trapped→both free | timid sanctioned escape→flight without return | forced gunshot, panic→the leap, hands joined, chosen. Accidental transgression→self-authored act. The pair also work as protagonist/antagonist swapping roles, like Richard II and Bolingbroke.
- **E.T.** ship leaves E.T. stranded→ship returns for him | Elliott alone, disbelieved→believed, surrounded | hides E.T. (isolating secret)→releases him (completing farewell).
- **Strictly Ballroom.** own steps condemned→own steps triumph | partner abandons, establishment closes ranks→establishment sabotages, Fran/family stand with him | dismisses Fran→chooses Fran, whole floor joins.

## VII. Lightning guide to the gurus

Beneath over-complication, bizarre terminology, blind certainty and demands for money, **all gurus articulate the same thing**: the strongest proof of the archetype is that every theory (Yorke's, Propp's, Campbell's) is identical. Comparisons are deliberately simplistic; originals reward reading.

- **Aristotle** (Poetics): whole with beginning/middle/end; peripeteia + anagnorisis → catharsis. Beginning=act1; middle=acts2-4 (recognition at midpoint); unravelling/end=act5.
- **Horace / Terence:** a play is neither shorter nor longer than five acts: the template itself.
- **Freytag** (1863): pyramid: intro, rising action, CLIMAX AT APEX, falling action, catastrophe; "exciting force." Exciting force = inciting incident; **apex = the midpoint** (his witness that "climax" is the middle, not the end); catastrophe = act5.
- **Propp** (1928): 31 fixed-sequence folktale functions, 7 roles (a morphologist, not a guru). Absentation/villainy/departure=acts1-2; struggle/victory=act3; return/pursuit=act4; recognition/false-hero/wedding=act5.
- **Campbell** (1949): monomyth: separation, initiation, return; hero assimilates his opposite, returns with the boon. Separation=acts1-2; supreme ordeal=act3 midpoint; return=act4; master of two worlds=act5. *Caveat:* myth didn't birth structure, structure birthed myth; the "nuclear unit" is just a scene.
- **Vogler** (1992): Campbell in 12 stages. Ordinary world/threshold=acts1-2; ordeal=midpoint; road back=act4; resurrection=act5; swap "elixir" for cure-of-flaw. *Caveats:* refusal isn't universal; "midpoint as death" over-rests on Psycho/E.T.; cultish packaging.
- **McKee** (1999): inciting incident, progressive complications, crisis, climax, resolution; the gap; controlling idea. Incident closes act1; complications=acts2-4; crisis=act4 worst point; climax/resolution=act5. *Caveat:* rebaptizes simple tenets with needless terms.
- **Field** (1979): three acts on Plot Points I & II, midpoint, two pinch points. PP1=act1/2 turn; midpoint=midpoint; **pinch points are really act breaks** (why his act 2 is three acts); PP2=act4 crisis; act III=act5.
- **Snyder** (Save the Cat, 2005): 15-beat sheet. Catalyst/debate=acts1-2; midpoint=midpoint; all-is-lost + dark-night=act4 worst point; break-into-three/finale=act5. *Caveat:* page-number dogmatism is blind certainty; beats are the archetype under marketing.
- **Truby** (2007): 22 steps on 7 keys (weakness/need, desire, opponent, plan, battle, self-revelation, new equilibrium). Weakness/desire=act1; opponent/plan=act2; battle=acts3-4; self-revelation/new equilibrium=act5. *Caveat:* the 7 keys are the five-act engine renamed.
- **Booker** (2004): seven plots on one meta-pattern: anticipation, dream, frustration, nightmare, resolution/destruction. Cleanest 1:1 (=acts1-5). *Caveat:* ignores the midpoint.
- **Egri** (1942): stated premise proven by action; orchestration + unity of opposites. Premise via flaw=act1; unity of opposites locks protagonist/antagonist acts2-4; premise proven=act5. *Caveat:* nearest true antecedent of the dialectical view, but insists one side must win (negates synthesis).
- **Murdock** (Heroine's Journey, 1990): corrective to Campbell for women: separation from the feminine, trials, aridity, descent, reconnection, integration. Separation/trials=acts1-2; illusory boon + descent=midpoint into act4 (aridity=worst point); reconnection/integration=act5. Same journey to assimilate the missing opposite, gendered.
- **Daniel** (eight-sequence): feature = 8 sequences of ~12-15 min, each a mini-movie. Seq 1-2=acts1-2; 3-4 close at midpoint; 5-6=decline to act4 crisis; 7-8=climax/resolution=act5. Fractal principle as pedagogy.

## The master key

Every model reduces to one engine: a protagonist leaves home (thesis), journeys into the woods to confront an opposite (antithesis), touches the central truth at the exact middle, pays for that knowledge at the worst point, returns home changed (synthesis). Act structure, roadmap of change, hero's journey, beat sheet, sequence method: all fractal enlargements of a single scene in which a character meets their opposite and assimilates it. **When mapping any new story, find the midpoint truth and the first/last-act mirror before anything else;** the rest follows as water finds its level.
