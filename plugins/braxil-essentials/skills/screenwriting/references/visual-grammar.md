# Visual Grammar: the Language of Cinema

Source: Filmmaker's Handbook (Ascher & Pincus, 4th ed.), ch. 9. Grammar of framing, camera movement, shot-to-shot flow. Style and content are inseparable; meaning in every shot.

## Core units
- **Scene**: event, one setting, continuous time (following an actor room-to-room often = new scene).
- **Sequence**: scene(s) forming a unit; may span locales into one whole.
- **Shot / camera angle**: a piece a scene splits into for editing (may be one shot); the take or the edited portion, per context.
- **Setup**: new camera position/angle (usually new lighting). Focal-length change from same spot ≠ new setup.
- **Take**: one camera run start-to-stop. "8A, Take 4" = 4th attempt at angle A of scene 8.
- **Master shot**: wide continuous take of entire scene action, filmed through.
- **Coverage**: additional angles beyond the master.
- **MOS**: shot without sound.

## Shot vocabulary
- **ECU**: part of face/small object fills frame.
- **Big CU**: whole face fills frame.
- **CU**: a detail; person = head-and-shoulders; focus the eyes.
- **Two-button CU**: face to 2nd shirt button.
- **Dirty CU**: CU with back of another head/element in FG.
- **MCU**: CU–MS, ~chest up.
- **MS**: head-to-knee or waist up.
- **Two-shot**: two actors front, knees/waist up.
- **Knee shot**: knees-up framing.
- **LS**: whole body vs. environment, from far.
- **WS**: wide space, environment dominant.
- **Establishing shot**: LS defining the space/locale.
- **Reverse-angle**: two shots from opposite angles.
- **OTS**: over one actor's shoulder; standard objective dialogue coverage.
- **POV shot**: from a subject's vantage (over shoulder at eye height, or extreme like bird's-eye).
- **Insert**: close detail within scene (hands, object, note).
- **Cutaway**: shot away from main action; covers discontinuity/condenses.
- **Reaction shot**: someone reacting; cheap editing insurance, always get some.
- **Shot/reverse shot**: conversation coverage, each alone in ¾ profile; one looks screen-right then the other screen-left; usually CUs, often dirty.
- **Deep-focus shot**: whole frame sharp; meaning in deep space.
- **Long take**: long duration; meaning from filming not editing.
- **B-roll**: noninterview material; plays sync a few seconds then audio dips under narration.

### Shot-size ladder (tight→wide)
ECU → Big CU → CU (head/shoulders, eyes) → Two-button CU → MCU (chest) → MS (waist; two people = two-shot) → Knee → LS (body+surroundings) → EWS/wide (figure small).

### Size flow
- Traditional **LS→MS→CU** orients to space; tighter shots suggest forward movement, delving deeper.
- Wider (MS→LS): expect larger-scale action, arrival or leave-taking (often film's end).
- Defaults, not laws; a scene can play entirely in ECU if earned. Classic: two-shot → MS of one → reverse angle. Orient, then alternate.

## Composition
Framing directs attention, excludes, satisfies. Unlike stills: subjects move, frame moves, edit-adjacency creates tensions over time; an off-balance frame can resolve via subject/camera movement or editing. Modes: **scripted** = framing blocked before take; **unscripted** = improvised from viewfinder + what's heard/seen off-frame.

### Static-frame rules
- **Rule of thirds**: avoid dead center (dull); interest ~⅓ from a side; in CU/MCU eyes ~⅓ from top, nose centered. Many good comps ignore it.
- **Headroom**: space above head; variable, subjective; natural in WS can feel excessive in CU. Go by instinct.
- **Edges**: place comfortably inside or cut off decisively, never fighting the edge; avoid dead space/clutter.
- **Backgrounds**: watch directly behind subject (plants "growing" from heads, distractions).
- **Expectations**: comps set expectations to surprise/confirm/deny; horror uses imbalance to imply off-frame threat; gross imbalance now deliberate flavor.

### Lead/looking room
Subject moving one way → place near the edge moved FROM, room ahead (profile looking screen-right: near left edge). In a continuing LS subject can advance and exit frame. Void behind the look = awkward, or signals someone approaching from behind (can be deliberate).

### Depth, scale, space
- Angle to reveal more sides for depth (head-on=1, angled=2, down+angled=3).
- Familiar reference for scale (person by boulder).
- **Deep-space** (Hollywood): angle/movement/light separate FG/BG, exclude flat black/white. **Flat** (European '60s-'70s): shoot perpendicular to walls, let areas over/underexpose. Both legit: choose on purpose.

### TV cutoff / safe areas / aspect
- Framed ≠ delivered: CRT enlarges + crops edges (overscan); web = full frame; panels vary. **Safe action** = may crop; **safe title** = closer to center for readable text. Unwanted objects may/may not show.
- Widescreen may show non-widescreen and vice versa; widescreen look often from shooting less-wide + cropping post (16:9→1.85/2.39); if so shoot a framing chart and tape the monitor. Leave bottom room for lower-thirds.

## Angles, height, eye lines
- **Low** → importance/stature (or unflattering); **high** → diminishes; **bird's-eye** → god-like/detached.
- **Dutch angle** (canted horizon) → tension/unease, often MCU; a Dutch head rig tilts during a take. Unleveled tripod = accidental cant; tilt only on purpose.
- **Camera height** matched to a character's position (low at person on couch, high from stander) quietly assigns the shot to their experience.

### Subjectivity ladder (eye line vs. lens axis)
Angle between eye line (direction looked) and lens controls subjectivity. Objective→direct:
1. Eye line far from lens; profile two-shots, OTS → objective.
2. Camera near actors' eye lines; clean singles (no shoulder); heights matched → heightened shared POV.
3. Eye line very near lens, not into it → subjective POV.
4. Into the lens → fourth-wall break / "inside the other's head"; easily awkward in drama (Lady in the Lake = whole film through protagonist's eyes).
- Interviews: interviewer near camera so subject looks toward-but-not-into lens. Morris's **interrotron** projects his face on the lens so the subject looks INTO it while talking to him. Hosts look into lens to address audience.
- **Eyeline match**: look off screen → cut to what's seen → reads POV instantly. One of the strongest tools.

## Lens & focus as storytelling
- **Deep focus** (wide lens, all sharp): meaning from staging not cutting.
- **Selective focus**: accentuates part; with comp+light pulls viewer in.
- **Rack/pulling focus** BG↔FG shifts attention; mannered unless following a move.
- **Tilt-focus lens**: tilts focus plane to isolate a narrow area.
- Lighting can change mid-shot to redirect attention.
- **Focal length**: wide: exaggerate depth/moving-through-space, hide jiggle; long: flatten/compress, slow apparent toward/away motion, can simulate a traveling camera. In CU focus the eyes.

## Moving camera
Static (locked-off) vs. moving; traveling through space = most cinematic, most difficult/expensive.
- **Pan**: pivot horizontal L/R; best motivated by a moving subject. Landscape pans hardest (unevenness shows; keep slow to avoid judder).
- **Tilt**: pivot vertical up/down; reveals height/scale/power, follows vertical action.
- **Dolly/track (in/out)**: travel toward/away on wheels; perspective changes, objects pass edges, viewer enters; creates depth.
- **Crab/truck (L/R)**: lateral alongside; walking characters, sliding past environments.
- **Boom/pedestal/crane (up/down)**: vertical travel of whole camera; dolly-with-boom expands lexicon; cranes/jibs reach for grand reveals/high angles.
- **Slider**: short rail glide (2-6 ft); even a slow glide adds value.
- **Handheld**: carried on body; reads interiority/subjectivity/urgency/doc realism.
- **Steadicam/stabilizer**: gimbal isolates body shake; dolly-smooth with handheld freedom.
- **Zoom**: focal-length change during shot, NOT movement; magnifies equally (like approaching a photo), flattens, can call attention to filming, picks out a detail.
- **Swish pan**: very fast blurring pan; energy/transition; blur hides judder.
- **Pop zoom**: sudden jump between focal lengths; punctuation/roughness.
- **Dolly-zoom**: zoom one way + dolly the other; field size constant, perspective warps → vertigo/dread.

### Zoom vs. dolly
- Zoom = closer without perspective change, flattens; dolly = perspective changes, objects pass edges, viewer enters, creates depth.
- **Treadmill effect**: zoom against subject/camera motion: runner toward camera as lens zooms back makes no progress.
- Graceful zoom: start slow, reach speed, ease to stop; "bury" a needed zoom inside a pan to hide it. Zoom only when motivated (novices are "zoom happy"). Cuts during a slow zoom often work. Motorized = slow/smooth (feather to stop); manual = direct feel, faster, rougher. In treadmill/dolly-zoom the perspective carries the emotion.

### Handheld doctrine
- Arrived with lightweight sync cameras early '60s; launched New Wave + cinema verite.
- Convention: wobble = interiority/subjectivity (though real gait is steady); panic/frenzy reinforced by shake; horror tremor = unseen onlooker.
- Extreme mobility → intimacy/spontaneity; best for unscripted/improvised; skilled operators can be steady; audiences accept occasional handheld among mounted shots.
- Craft: wide lenses hide jiggle; walking = bent knees + shuffle to avoid bob; static handheld on landscapes/architecture betrays jiggle (mount it).

### Steadicam & stabilizers
- Handheld freedom + dolly/crane control; any vehicle = dolly-smooth platform; pans/tilts/runs/stairs with human subtlety.
- Character: floating (some find less exciting than good handheld); slower to unplanned motion than shoulder-mount; level-horizon at move's end is hard; wide lenses best tracking. Often combined (Steadicam/slider/dolly/crane/boom) for flow with no character POV.

### Support palette (can / constraint)
- **Tripod+fluid head**: smooth pans/tilts, drag eases panning; static, must level.
- **Geared head**: smooth repeatable for heavy cameras; heavy, needs skill.
- **Hi hat/table stand**: very low angles; static.
- **Doorway/western dolly**: cheap portable travel; steerable, no lateral.
- **Crab dolly+boom**: any direction + vertical; needs smooth floor/plywood/track.
- **Dolly on track**: smoothest/repeatable; curved track circles subject at constant distance; track must be laid/leveled/hidden, always ends.
- **Jib/crane**: high angles/reach/reveals; harder than built-in booms; cherry pickers raise but can't end a move cleanly.
- **Slider**: precise 2-6 ft; length-limited.
- **Handheld**: mobility/intimacy, absorbs vehicle vibration; shake reads as subjectivity regardless, tiring.
- **Steadicam**: dolly-smooth anywhere a person walks; floating, slower reaction, end-of-move horizon control.
- **Camera vehicle/tow rig**: tracking faster than any dolly, picture car towed so actors act; bigger=smoother, constant speed easiest, suction mounts need safety lines.
- **Gyro/heli mounts**: stabilized from boats/aircraft.
- **Software stabilization**: post rescue (bouncy→smooth); crops/warps, a net not a plan.
- Even without moving shots, a dolly+boom saves set time; the dolly grip needs operator finesse.

### Movement rules
- **Shape of a move**: static start, gain/hold speed, ease to stop; begin/end on held frames (editor may trim, but they exist). Often broken; constant-speed moves exist.
- **Every shot/move needs a shape** (beginning + end); constant motion is hard to cut.
- **Foreground rule**: a move reads strongest passing near-FG objects; wide lenses emphasize.
- **Push-in**: dolly in underscores dawning realization/seriousness.
- **Camera as character**: camera prowls/manipulates; a continuous track over objects builds tension impossible in cuts.
- **Screen size**: bigger projection exaggerates every move (pan fine on phone disorients in theater).
- **Directorial questions**: how should the audience experience it? favor a POV? distant or close? voyeur or participant? lurch/creep/weave? Use blocking to reveal, use mystery; some shots best for what they DON'T show.

## Style: mise-en-scene vs. montage
- **Mise-en-scene** (staging): deep focus/long take, whole scene develops in-frame; movement/dialogue/light/costume carry it. Bazin: integrity of photographed space = advanced direction (Keaton stunts in LS are clearly real; powers documentary).
- **Montage**: many shorter shots: control pacing, direct attention, build new meaning by juxtaposition.
- Two tools, not opposites: edited = impact; long master = self-discovery. Extremes: a single ~3-min static dialogue master; a 3.5-min continuous crane opening take.

### Classic Hollywood grammar
- Griffith first exploited the CU → new audience/actor bond, subtler acting; silent cinema defined the image vocabulary.
- To 1950s: shoot master through, then CUs; edited scene opens on the wide to orient before cutting closer.
- **Four-setup coverage** of a two-person scene: **master, two-shot, CU of one, reverse CU of the other**. Radical exception: entirely-subjective POV.
- Since '60s loosened: ECU scenes, doc/narrative blend, music-video cutting, deliberate shake/distortion/jump cuts, mockumentary; mainstream drama still keeps style "invisible". Training: watch noting what works; sound-off to isolate camera-editing.

### Four-setup dialogue plan
A and B across the eye-line axis (180 line); all four setups ONE side: [1] master establishes, [2] two-shot carries movement, [3] CU of A over B's shoulder + [4] CU of B over A's shoulder alternate as shot/reverse shot.

## POV in scene construction
Whose experience a sequence follows is a SCRIPT decision before camera; POV = writing + directing + editing, decide before production. Same events (man gets bad results, goes home), three coverages:
1. Camera with the man → identify with him.
2. Camera with the wife (office visit skipped) → her experience.
3. Open on doctor reacting; man enters, cut to him silent at dinner → audience knows before the character; later scenes gain irony/tension; emotion via expression in little time.

- **Restricted POV**: noir: stay with the detective, gather clues with him, no access to what he doesn't witness.
- **Audience knowledge sets each shot's value**: a shot suspenseful when we know the stakes is dull when we don't. Plan coverage per audience knowledge.
- **Hitchcock surprise vs. suspense**: the bomb-under-the-table mechanics (surprise vs suspense per what the audience knows) live in scene-craft.md "Surprise vs suspense".

## Continuity style (illusion of continuous time)
Single-camera drama films one angle, moves, restages (across days/locations). **Continuity style** = editing shots filmed at different times so action flows ("invisible cutting"). Some scenes demand it, some don't.

### 180-degree rule
Screen direction = L/R as audience sees (subject facing camera moving to HIS left moves screen right). The line (director's line) preserves it across cuts. Draw a line through the action axis (car, field, conversation eye line); keep all setups ONE side → direction preserved. (Football: camera across the field flips the team.)
- **Legal crossings**: (1) shots ON the line are neutral, cut either side (into camera; straight down axis); (2) camera may cross visibly DURING a take (audience travels with it), but if that take is cut you're stuck violating.
- Violations disorient but rarely disastrous; a neutral shot minimizes it. Critical where direction builds space: shot/reverse speakers must look OPPOSITE ways or stop conversing; chases need consistent direction to read as pursuit not collision.
- **Conflicting lines**: car couple angle/reverse: BG moves opposite behind each; relationship is key → draw the line on their eye line, let BG contradiction stand.
- **Across scenes**: interviews frame opposing subjects (filmed apart) facing camera-left vs. -right; intercut reads as opposition.

### 30-degree rule & jump cuts
Same framing of same person = **jump cut**. Avoid: change BOTH size AND angle (≥30°), size via focal length/distance + move laterally. WS cuts easily with MS/CU; two MS feel unsmooth. Smoothness also depends on action, background, especially sound continuity. Jump cuts now routine: mark a time jump or just shorten.

### Match on action / overlapping action
- Restaged action consistent take to take (cup lifted on the same line every angle).
- **Overlap action across setups** even when each part is one angle: shoot the WS through the door-slam, start the CU from before the slam and repeat → editor cuts on action at several points. Move laterally between setups to smooth and hide mismatch. Cut around unintentional mismatch (attention elsewhere); digital: check the previous take.

### Eyeline match
Look off screen → next shot = what's seen from ~their vantage → audience binds them; a POV shot then cut retroactively assigns shot 1 to the looker, then shot/reverse tightening.

### Exit frame
Character walks off camera → viewer accepts a time jump (out the door, cut to street feels continuous). When tracking a mover, let him exit at shot's end = clean exit, more options.

### Matching state
Gesture/prop/wardrobe/weather/hair/light carry across every angle: the script supervisor's job. Boarding: treat prop/costume state as continuity data on the scene, not the shot.

### Continuity without control (doc logic)
- Can't restage but continuity rules: know what the current shot can cut with; change angle AND focal length when a cut's needed.
- Repeated action (cooking, chopping): cover from angles + overlaps.
- Concealment for editing freedom: OTS behind the talker, phone blocking lips, neutral musician shots with no finger positions → any lip/sync-hiding shot overlays any audio.

## Compressing & expanding time
Give screen time to interest/meaning/emotion; move fast through dull-but-necessary points; rewrite hunting what to cut.

### Shorten within continuity
- **Dissolve**: bridge between distant moments.
- **Reveal**: end shot 1 wide; begin shot 2 on a CU and pull back to reveal.
- **Cutaway bridge**: cut away and back (thing further along); also condenses a speech.
- **Jump cut**: accepted to shorten.
- **Exit frame**: leave, next shot later/elsewhere.

### Extend time
- Block/cover so action earns time.
- **Slow motion (overcranking)**: camera runs faster, slowed motion stays smooth; extends real time, adds weight. Now oversprayed, use with intent.
- Post-slowing normal footage repeats frames → jerky, visible blur; interpolation helps; shoot fast shutter if planning post slow-mo; true overcranking smoother.
- Mixing slow-mo/sped-up/lingering/short shots → extends AND compresses at once.

### High-speed extremes
- Very high fps captures fleeting events (droplet, bullet through glass); high-speed digital = thousands fps; strobe = ultrashort shutter.
- Scale: 250 fps stretches 1 real sec → 10+ sec at 24p; board accordingly. Needs lots of light. Smooth blur-free slow motion comes only from true high fps, never post-slowing.
- Side use: on handheld/moving vehicles, faster camera speed lengthens jerk gaps → steadier.

### Fast motion & time-lapse
- **Undercranking** (below normal) speeds motion; chases ~16-20 fps at 24 look faster/dangerous. Buys exposure in low light if nothing moves (12 fps = 1 stop over 24: actors/camera at half speed, playback normal); slowed in post → ghostlike.
- **Time-lapse** compresses hours→seconds. Math from desired time: 2-hr sunset for 10s at 24p = 240 frames ≈ 2 frames/min. Start before/end after, shoot extra; fewer/farther exposures = jumpier. Wide lens best (traffic = river; long lens = cars popping per frame). Walk/dolly a step per frame → moving time-lapse. Slow per-frame shutter = more blur (streak night lights). Decide fixed exposure (base on key part; auto-iris can silhouette wrong) vs. "riding" the light.
- **Pixilation/animation**: time-lapse variants: expose few frames, nudge subject, repeat; still things self-move.
- **Speed ramp**: normal → ramp to high speed as key action begins (auto exposure), for a fleeting event inside a normal shot.

## Motion smoothness (boarding fast action)
Too much change per frame = **judder** (strobing/skipping; eyestrain).
- At 24 fps, fast pans/subject motion judder. Rule: allow **5-7 sec** for an object to cross the screen (pan OR subject through still frame).
- Worst panning across strong verticals; higher contrast/sharpness worse, avoid.
- Panning WITH a subject hides BG judder (shallow DOF, focus subject, soften BG). Swish pans fine (blur hides all).
- 60 fps fairly smooth; judder is a 24 fps problem but can appear at higher rates with a very fast shutter. Small-sensor judders more (harder to blur BG); slower shutter reduces it. Bigger screen = worse; often worse in viewfinder than monitor.
- **Wagon-wheel effect**: wheels appear stopped/backward when exposures catch spokes at same/earlier positions per frame.

### Vehicle speed on screen
- Along travel → normal; at right angles → ~TWICE as fast; intermediate → between; wide lens faster, long lens slower.
- Plausibility: bigger car smoother; constant speed easiest, standing start hardest; higher fps smooths ride; tow the picture car so actors act; side-window "dolly" shot from the tow vehicle.

## Quick reference: intent → tool
- New space → establishing then closer (LS→MS→CU).
- Deeper in → tighter sizes; motivated push-in.
- Larger event / farewell → wider (MS→LS).
- Objective conversation → profile two-shot + OTS shot/reverse.
- Shared intimacy → clean singles, eye lines near lens, heights matched.
- Full subjectivity → eye line almost into lens; POV + eyeline matches.
- Direct address / rupture → into the lens (fourth wall).
- Dawning realization → slow push-in on face.
- Power/menace → low angle, looming height.
- Vulnerability/smallness → high angle, distance.
- Unease/instability → Dutch angle, imbalance, empty space behind a look.
- Lurking unseen → off-balance frame, handheld tremor.
- Panic/frenzy → shaky handheld.
- Lyrical flow, no owner → Steadicam/dolly/crane/slider combined.
- Physically enter a space → dolly in (never zoom).
- Snap attention to a detail → zoom, rack focus (follow a move), insert.
- Vertigo/wrongness → dolly-zoom.
- Two ideas fused → montage of juxtaposed shorter shots.
- Unfaked reality/virtuosity → long take, deep focus, staging in depth.
- Suspense not surprise → show the bomb first.
- Opposition between speakers who never meet → opposing screen directions.
- Time quietly removed → cutaway bridge, dissolve, reveal, exit frame, jump cuts.
- Moment made monumental → overcranked slow motion.
- Chaos accelerated → undercranking, fast cutting.
- Hours in seconds → time-lapse, wide lens.
- Car conversation cutting cleanly → 180 line on the couple's eye line, not the road.
- Pursuit that reads as pursuit → consistent screen direction every cut.
